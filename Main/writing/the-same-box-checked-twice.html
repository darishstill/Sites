The Same Box, Checked Twice

Darish K. Still · November 2026

I believe that if you only treat the problem someone walks in with, and never what's underneath it, you haven't helped them. You've only managed the symptom.

I work as a case manager in substance use treatment, and part of my job is reading a person's whole file: the intake assessment, the group notes, the treatment plan. That's where I see this most clearly. The assessment records the trauma. The notes show the person showing up week after week. The plan even names trauma as part of what brought them in. But every goal is about coping with the symptoms. None of them is about healing the trauma itself.

More than once, I've opened the chart of someone returning to treatment and compared their two intakes side by side. The trauma history is almost word for word the same: the same box checked on the same line, months apart. Nothing in between touched it. We wrote that wound down twice and treated it neither time.

This doesn't only happen in addiction treatment. It happens when someone is sent home from the hospital to the same unstable housing and the same untreated depression that brought them to the ER three times this year. It happens when a family's case is closed without anyone asking how they reached a crisis in the first place. The problem people show up with is rarely the whole story.

So when someone comes back, I don't see failure. I see a message: the same line, checked twice, never once addressed.

If you've been through treatment more than once, I want you to hear that clearly. Coming back doesn't mean you didn't try hard enough. It may mean the deepest part of what you carry never got the attention it needed. That's the work I'm here for: answering what's in that box, not just the reason someone walked through the door. Otherwise, we haven't helped. We've only given the cycle somewhere safe to rest before it starts again.
